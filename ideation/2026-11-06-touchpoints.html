Who meets which screen, when, and what they change

Design by where in a person's day we interrupt, not by how many screens there are. Alongside that, the “three things we need decided” are split down to a grain you can answer with a plain yes or no. If Stack-chan goes in, which single scene it belongs to, and which scene runs on which device (phone, PC, or the physical device), are written down too.

2026-11-06kouAssumes: 5 screens; the transition table is in screen-flow.html

Executive summary

  1. The value of this product is that, most of the time, nothing appears. The holder looks at a screen once a day at most. Any proposal that increases the number of interruptions is itself a failure.
  2. There are only three moments where interrupting is allowed. When a heavy request arrives (press) / when the flame goes out (vouch again) / when you find out afterwards that something was stopped (the trail).
  3. Only the moments that touch the key are tied to a device. The only thing that can touch the physical device is a process on the PC the device is plugged into (bridge listens on 127.0.0.1). Everything else is Web, opened on a phone. We build no new native app.
  4. If Stack-chan goes in, it is the signal for “a heavy request arrived” and nothing else. It holds neither judgement nor approval. A signal is meaningless unless it comes from the physical device, but the place you press stays the device that holds the key.

03Which scene runs on which device (hypothesis)

Only the scenes that touch the key are tied to a device. Everything else is Web, opened on a phone. Not one new native app gets built.

The constraint comes from exactly one place. What talks to the physical device is bridge/bridge.py; it speaks USB HID and listens only on 127.0.0.1. So the only thing that can touch the physical device is a process on the machine it is plugged into. A path for pressing from a phone browser does not exist in the current implementation. Everything else follows from this.

③ alone may split “the device you notice on” from “the device you decide on”

A heavy request arrives while the holder is not looking at a screen. So put the noticing on something carried, or something in the line of sight (a phone, or a Stack-chan on the desk), and keep the deciding on the device that holds the key. Splitting them is safer, and cheaper too — the noticing side must not be able to let anything through, so it only needs to read. It needs neither a key nor a signature.

If one thing is to be decided, it is this. Where ③'s notice comes from (A do not show it / B a phone notification / C a signal from the physical device). The default is A (not shown this time). A signal that misfires makes a product that talks about trust lose trust by its own hand.

04If Stack-chan goes in, which scene

The signal for ③ “a heavy request arrived”, and that alone. It is given no judgement, no approval, no key.

① First permitthe screen's job
② Everydaystays silent
③ Heavy requestturns to you and tells you
④⑤ Stoppedthe trail, later
⑦ The flame went outtells you once

Why ③. A heavy request arrives while the holder is not looking at a screen. A countdown or notification put on a device screen does not reach someone who is not looking. A signal is meaningless unless it comes from the physical device (we made this same mistake on 2026-09-12: nobody at the venue looked at a device). Stack-chan sits on the desk, can turn towards you, glow, and say one short line. That one point is the thing a screen cannot do.

SceneWhat Stack-chan doesWhat it must not do
③ Heavy requestTurns towards you/glows flame-coloured/says one line: “it is being asked to send $4,800”Never approves. Never lets a nod or a voice let something through (do not break “press on the device that holds the key”)
③ AfterwardsGoes dark once pressed. If it is not pressed, it waits in silenceNever nags repeatedly. Rush a person and they press without reading what it says
⑦ The flame went outTells you once (“the version changed and the flame went out”)Does not demand a re-light on the spot. World is the job of the owner's own device
④⑤ StoppedDoes nothingDoes not announce that it stopped something. Stopping is normal, and announcing it every time is just noise
② EverydayDoes nothingNo “I am watching over you” theatrics

Even if it goes in, we are not building it this time. There is no time before submission, and if the signal misfires, a product that talks about trust loses trust by its own hand. For now we keep only the design position — “③'s signal comes from the physical device” — and do not mention it in the video either. If asked, we answer out loud (it is the question most likely to come up at the booth, so we answer straight from this table).
