The architecture, and the five flows

The design written out in prose (inputs and outputs, screen flow, market) turned into lines and order. Five flows: passes, requires a press, stops, declines to decide, lights a flame. The diagrams are not drawn by hand; they are generated from ideation/build-diagrams.py (change a flow by changing the script).

2026-11-06kouImplementation: broker/src/server.js, bridge/bridge.py

Executive summary

  1. One gate, four answers. Let through / not until it is pressed / stopped / could not decide, so stopped. Never inventing a fifth answer is the core of the design.
  2. Keys and bodies do not cross the gate. The key never leaves the device, and the request body stays inside the 0G seal. The Broker receives a weight without reading the content.
  3. A person appears in two places only. The long press on a heavy request, and the single sign-in that lights a flame. No person appears in the routine flow — that there is no person in diagram 01 is the answer.

01Architecture

Left is the near side, the middle is the gate, the right is the parties we need not trust. Trust is required at one point only, and nothing is deposited there.

02Flow 01 — A routine request — it passes in silence

A request inside scope that 0G read as routine. The person does nothing. No screen appears.

03Flow 02 — A heavy request — nothing passes until it is pressed

A request 0G read as decisive does not pass on a standing permit. It needs a single-use permit — a press on the spot.

04Flow 03 — Outside scope — stopped, and recorded as a violation

A request outside the declared scope is recorded together with the skill's own signature. 0G is not asked (it is already out of scope).

05Flow 04 — 0G unreachable — stop instead of deciding

No default is created. "Probably light, let it through" and "stop just in case" are both false verdicts.

06Flow 05 — Lighting a flame — naming yourself once with World

Not asked on every use of the key. World appears only when a flame is lit.

07When the version rises, the flames go out

This is the specification, not a fault. Because they go out, you can see whether a skill is in use now.

What this means for the buyer (P3). Zero flames means "a new version that nobody is using yet". It is a device for not mistaking an old rating for a current one, not a punishment for the maker.
