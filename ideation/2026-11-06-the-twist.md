# Who do we get empathy from, and how much / where to put the "oh, that's where you're going"

**Let's state the weakness of the previous proposal (`2026-11-06-journey-and-story.md`) first.**

**That one only gets "recognition".** "The 0.5 seconds before pasting an API key" is
an experience almost everyone in the room has had, but **it ends at "yeah, I know that" and is gone in 3 seconds.**
And **"not the number of stars, but whose star it is" is already a known idea**
(web of trust, the circle of people you follow). **Well made, but no surprise.**

**Empathy with no surprise is not remembered.** → replaced in §2.

---

## 1. From whom, and how much, can we get empathy

**Don't say "empathy from lots of people" in one breath. The kind and the depth differ per person.**

| Who | Count | Experience they already have | Reaction we can get | Depth | Persistence |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| Developers in the room | Hundreds | Pasted a key / afraid of updates | **Recognition**: "yeah, I know" | Shallow | **3 seconds** |
| **Judges** | **~20** | **They see 60 of these a day. They are bored** | **Surprise only. Empathy barely works** | — | — |
| **People who try things first** (dense in the room) | Tens to a hundred | **They have been the guinea pig with nobody praising them** | **Acknowledgement**: "it finally has a name" | **Deep** | **It stays** |
| People who watch the video later | Broad | — | Shallow | Shallow | — |

### The real target is "the people who go first"

**The people in the room are the ones who install new things first, break them, and fix them.**
They **have been unpaid guinea pigs forever, and nobody has ever called them that.**

**Being told "what you have been doing has a name and a value" is
acknowledgement, not recognition.** That does not vanish in 3 seconds.

**And judges are not moved by empathy. Only by surprise.** Design for them separately.

## 1.5 The entrance to empathy is where you put the key (in his own words, 2026-11-06)

> **"You keep wanting to hand over your API key — where am I supposed to put this...
> and you end up just dumping it in. I think that gets empathy."**

**This is more correct than "the 0.5 seconds before pressing Enter" that I had written.**
That one was about **hesitating, then pressing**. The real reason is further upstream.

### There is no place to put it

| Where to put it | Why it doesn't work |
|---|---|
| `.env` | **Right next to the repository.** Forget `.gitignore` once and it's over |
| Environment variables | **Every child process can read them.** Including processes the AI spawned |
| 1Password / Keychain | Safe. **But the agent can't read them, so you copy and paste it anyway** |
| Written directly into a config file | Easiest, and worst |
| **Entrust it to the tool** | **The only one that actually works** |

> **Every safe place to put it is unusable.
> Every usable place to put it is unsafe.
> So people pick the unsafe one.**

**This is not laziness.** **They do it because it is the only thing that works.**
**Everyone in the room did this this week.**

**Do not blame them.** The moment you say "let's be careful", you become the enemy.
**Say "that really is the only option, isn't it."**

### And this is where the first inversion comes

> **I thought it was a problem of where to put it.**
> **It was not a problem of where to put it.**
>
> Wherever you put it, **if the thing you handed it to changes, it's the same.**
> The question is not the place; it is
> **whether what you handed it to is the same thing it was yesterday.**

**"Where do I put the key" is the wrong question.**
**The right question is "is what I handed it to still what it was when I handed it over".**

**This leads into §2.**
**You cannot answer that question yourself. Only the person who went first can.**

## 1.7 The fastest explanation: GitHub stars (in his own words, 2026-11-06)

> **"The star count is close to the feeling of a GitHub star.
> Except the weight of one vote is properly sorted out."**

**This is the fastest one.** Everyone in the room knows GitHub stars,
**and everyone also knows they're broken.** No explanation needed.

### It fits in one sentence

> **It's a GitHub star.
> Except you can only give one while you're running it with your own key,
> and it goes out when you stop.**

**That conveys the entire product.** Everything below is just a breakdown of that one sentence.

### What's different

| GitHub star | This star |
|---|---|
| **Pressing it is free** | **You can't press it unless you're using that version with your own key** |
| Once pressed it never goes away | **It goes out when you stop using it** |
| Unrelated to the version | **When the version changes it goes out for everyone at once** |
| **Only the count is visible** (nobody really looks at who pressed it) | **Who pressed it is the whole point. We don't show the count** |
| **Buyable** (star farms are real) | One per person (World) |
| **A bookmark** (huge numbers of people pressed it as "read later") | **The fact that someone is inside, right now** |

**A GitHub star is a record of "I looked". This star is a record of "I'm using it".**

### What "the weight of one vote is sorted out" means

**Weight = closeness x reality x present tense.**

| | Heavy | Light |
|---|---|---|
| **Closeness** | Someone you know pressed it | Someone you don't know pressed it |
| **Reality** | Using it with their own key | They only looked at it |
| **Present tense** | **Using it now** | **Used it long ago (= it's already gone out)** |

**GitHub stars crush all three down to 1 and add them up.**
**Which is why you can't compare 40,000 stars with 40 stars.**

### This makes the market statement concrete

**"43,012 MCP servers, and all they have is GitHub stars."**

**And the one choosing what to install is no longer a human.**
**If an AI is counting the stars, an AI will also be making the stars.**

## 2. "Oh, that's where you're going" — the star is not a recommendation, it's a hostage

### The assumption we hadn't noticed

**The structural flaw in reviews is not that there are lies in them.**

> **The person who wrote it is already gone.**
> They received the value, wrote, and left. **Their remaining risk is zero.**
> **Their influence begins exactly where their risk ended.**

**This is the shape of every review.** Five stars, likes, all the same.

### Our star

**The star is not "I think it's good".
It is the ongoing fact that "right now, with my own key, I am using this version".**

- **It goes out when you stop using it**
- **When the version changes it goes out for everyone at once**
- **In other words, a star shines because that person is still inside**

> **A review is written by someone who already left.
> This star means someone is still inside.**

**Not a recommendation, a hostage.**
**Not an endorsement, the fact that they haven't fled yet.**

### And here is the real inversion

The current script shows the scene where the stars go out all at once
as **"Priya was protected"**. **Weak.**

**The stars going out means someone came out ahead of her.**

Priya is unharmed because someone stood in front of her, and **that person noticed first and got out.**
**With exactly the same footage, a UI event becomes a human event.**

### So what is this work, really

**It is not a recommendation mechanism. It is safety equipment for the people who go first.**

> **Unless someone goes first, nobody can know.
> So we built a mechanism that brings the ones who go first back alive.**

**The three circles are not "proof that the star is genuine".**
**They are the equipment that makes going first something you can survive.**

| | Role as equipment |
|---|---|
| **Ledger** | **The lifeline of the person going first.** The AI can't pull it |
| **World** | **One person can only go first as one person.** You can't send a thousand fake canaries |
| **0G** | **Watches the air inside without reading the letters** |
| **The star going out** | **The canary came out. Everyone behind sees it at the same time** |

**The receiving side (Priya) is saved without doing anything.**
**The real customer of this work is the person who goes first.**
